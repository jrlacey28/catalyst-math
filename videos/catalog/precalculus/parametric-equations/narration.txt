Parametric equations give both coordinates in terms of a shared parameter. Let the horizontal coordinate equal t and the vertical coordinate equal t squared. As t varies, these paired outputs trace the familiar parabola while retaining an order of travel.

Evaluate both rules at the same parameter value. Negative two gives negative two, four; zero gives the origin; and positive two gives two, four. Mixing coordinates from different parameter values would create a point that the motion never actually visits at that time.

A different pair of rules traces a circle. Cosine and sine automatically satisfy x squared plus y squared equals one. As the parameter increases from zero to two pi, the point travels counterclockwise through one complete turn.

Doubling the parameter inside both functions traces the same geometric circle twice as quickly. Eliminating the parameter reveals the circle equation but loses this timing information. A curve can therefore have many parametrizations with different directions, speeds, or numbers of traversals.
