A field permits addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by every nonzero element. Rational numbers have this property. Zero remains excluded from division: multiplying zero by any candidate inverse still gives zero, never the required identity one.

Finite fields are possible too. Modulo the prime five, three has inverse two because their product six leaves remainder one. To solve three x equals four modulo five, multiply by two and reduce eight to three.

Check the result directly: three times three is nine, with remainder four. Modulo a prime, every nonzero remainder has an inverse. For composite moduli that statement can fail, so being a remainder system alone does not guarantee a field.

A field also determines which polynomial equations have solutions within it. The real numbers contain no square root of negative one. Extending to complex numbers adds i and its negative as roots, while keeping valid field arithmetic.
