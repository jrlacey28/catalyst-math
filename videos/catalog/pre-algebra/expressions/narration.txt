An expression describes a quantity without necessarily asserting an equation to solve. Two copies of the quantity x plus three means that both the variable part and the three additional units are repeated twice.

Distribution accounts for those repeated parts separately. Two copies of x contribute two x, and two copies of three contribute six. The rewritten expression gives the same output for every real input, as the overlapping graphs illustrate.

Like terms count the same variable quantity, so two x plus another x becomes three x. The six remains a separate constant. Adding coefficients is justified because the repeated units are identical, not because all symbols look similar.

A leading minus means multiplication by negative one, and it affects every term inside the parentheses. The two sides again represent one function. Simplifying preserves all inputs; solving for a particular input requires an additional condition.
