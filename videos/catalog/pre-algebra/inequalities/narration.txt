An inequality describes a set of allowed inputs rather than necessarily one solution. If x plus two must stay below five, subtracting two from both sides shows that every input below three works.

Including equality changes the boundary. When the condition says at most five, input three is allowed because it produces exactly five. A filled boundary point includes that input, whereas an open boundary point excludes it.

Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses order. Dividing negative two x less than six by negative two gives x greater than negative three. Reflection across zero explains why the direction of the comparison must switch.

Always test a value from the proposed interval. Zero is greater than negative three, and its original left side is zero, which is less than six. Checking a value outside the interval also helps detect a reversed inequality.
