A word problem becomes manageable when quantities are named before calculating. A delivery service charges five dollars plus two dollars per kilometer. Let d measure distance in kilometers and c measure the total charge in dollars.

For a four-kilometer delivery, the variable part is eight dollars, then the fixed fee adds five. The total thirteen dollars includes both contributions. Units prevent mistaking the five-dollar fee for five additional kilometers.

A nineteen-dollar bill reverses the question. Remove the fixed five-dollar fee to isolate fourteen dollars of distance charges, then divide by two dollars per kilometer. The resulting seven has units of kilometers, matching the unknown we named.

Finally check the model against the story. Nonnegative distances make the total at least five dollars, so a three-dollar bill cannot fit this pricing rule. A mathematically computed answer may be invalid if it violates the context or its units.
