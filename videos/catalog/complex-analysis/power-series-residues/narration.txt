Inside the unit disk, a geometric power series equals one divided by one minus z. Multiplying a finite partial sum by one minus z leaves one minus a small remainder. The condition on absolute value makes that remainder vanish.

Near a singularity, a Laurent expansion may also contain negative powers. The coefficient of one over z is called the residue at zero. For this simple pole, multiply by z and evaluate the remaining continuous factor at zero.

The residue at zero is negative one half. Around the positively oriented unit circle, zero lies inside and the other pole, two, lies outside. The residue theorem gives two pi i times the enclosed residue, hence negative pi i.

A counterclockwise circle of radius three encloses both simple poles. Their residues cancel, so that contour integral is zero. The rule assumes isolated singularities inside and none on the contour; a path through a pole requires different treatment.
