A contour integral adds changes along a directed path. Parameterize the path by a real variable t. Multiply the function value by the complex velocity of the path, then integrate in t; both path shape and orientation are accounted for.

For the function z, an antiderivative is z squared divided by two. Every piecewise smooth path from zero to one plus i therefore gives i. The endpoint formula follows because this antiderivative exists throughout the region containing the path.

Now travel counterclockwise around the unit circle while integrating one over z. The path velocity is i times z, so the product simplifies to i. Integrating from zero to two pi gives two pi i, not zero.

There is no contradiction with Cauchy's theorem: one over z is undefined at the enclosed origin. A holomorphic function on an open region containing the whole disk has zero integral around its boundary. Enclosed singularities change the situation.
