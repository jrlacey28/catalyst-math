Slope measures output change per unit input change along a nonvertical line. Choose two distinct points, compare their vertical coordinates, and divide by their horizontal separation. The order of subtraction must stay consistent in numerator and denominator.

From negative one, one to three, nine, the horizontal change is four and the vertical change is eight. Eight divided by four gives slope two: each step right corresponds to a rise of two on this line.

A downward trend gives a negative slope. Moving from zero, three to two, negative one changes height by negative four across a positive run of two. The sign records direction, while the magnitude records steepness relative to the axis units.

A vertical line has zero horizontal change, so its slope quotient would divide by zero. That slope is undefined, not zero. Zero slope belongs to a horizontal line, where the height remains unchanged across a nonzero horizontal movement.
