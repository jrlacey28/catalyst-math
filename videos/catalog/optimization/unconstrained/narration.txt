Unconstrained optimization searches without an additional feasible-set restriction. For this quadratic, the derivative points uphill. Moving a small distance opposite its sign moves toward the minimum at two, where the derivative is zero.

Gradient descent subtracts a step size times the derivative. With step size one quarter and starting point zero, the next input is one. Repeating gives one and a half, then one and three quarters: the error halves each time.

The error formula explains the step-size requirement for this example. Its magnitude contracts only when the absolute value of one minus twice alpha is below one. Thus alpha between zero and one converges; larger choices can make the iterates diverge.

A zero derivative is a candidate condition, not a universal minimum certificate. The cubic has derivative zero at the origin but takes smaller values immediately to its left. Use additional curvature or structural arguments to decide what a stationary point represents.
