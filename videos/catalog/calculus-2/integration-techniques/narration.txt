A useful integration technique recognizes the structure that differentiation would create. Here the outside is a power, the inside is x squared plus two, and the nearby factor two x is exactly the inside's derivative.

Name the inside u. The entire combination two x times d x becomes d u, so the integral becomes u cubed with respect to u. This substitution reverses the chain rule; it does not simply delete an inconvenient factor.

Integrating u cubed gives u to the fourth divided by four, plus a constant. Substitute the original inside back into the answer. Differentiating this result recreates both the outer cube and the required factor two x.

Products may call for integration by parts, the reverse of the product rule. Choose u equal to x and d v equal to exponential x d x. Subtracting the remaining exponential integral gives an answer you can verify by differentiating.
