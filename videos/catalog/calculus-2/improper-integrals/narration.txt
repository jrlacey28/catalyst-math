An infinite interval does not fit directly into an ordinary bounded integral. Replace infinity with a finite endpoint R, compute the area up to R, and then ask whether those accumulated areas approach a finite limit.

The antiderivative is negative one over x. Subtracting endpoint values gives one minus one over R. As R grows, the missing amount approaches zero, so this infinite tail has finite total area equal to one.

A shrinking height does not guarantee a finite total. For one over x, the accumulated area is the natural logarithm of R, which grows without bound. Both curves approach zero, but their tails accumulate at different rates.

Improper behavior can also occur at a finite endpoint. One over square root of x becomes unbounded near zero, yet its integral from epsilon to one is two minus twice square root of epsilon. The limit is finite.
