Let A record the area accumulated under the rate two t. Increasing the upper endpoint by a tiny amount adds a thin strip. Its width is the input change, and its height is approximately the current rate.

The thin-strip reasoning connects accumulation with differentiation. Here the area function is x squared, and its derivative returns two x. At input two, the accumulated amount changes at rate four, matching the original rate at that endpoint.

The other direction turns an integral into an endpoint subtraction. Since x squared differentiates to two x, subtract its value at one from its value at three. The change in accumulated area is nine minus one, or eight.

The pointwise derivative statement requires suitable regularity: continuity of the rate at the point is enough. At a jump, the accumulation can remain continuous but develop a corner. The theorem's assumptions explain where this direct identification works.
