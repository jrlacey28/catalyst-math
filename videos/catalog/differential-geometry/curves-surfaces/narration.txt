A parametrized curve describes position as a parameter changes. This formula traces a circle of radius two. Differentiating gives the velocity vector; its length is two, so equal changes in the parameter correspond to twice that much traveled arc length.

Speed depends on parameterization, while the geometric path remains the same. Using arc length s as the parameter makes speed one. The unit tangent then records direction alone, separating how the curve turns from how quickly it is traversed.

Curvature measures the rate at which the unit tangent changes per unit arc length. A radius-two circle has curvature one half; a larger circle turns less sharply. This is curve curvature, not the Gaussian curvature assigned to a surface.

A surface uses two parameters instead of one. On this paraboloid, changing u or v gives two independent tangent directions. Their span is the tangent plane. Independence of these parameter derivatives is the regularity condition preventing a collapsed coordinate patch.
