An eigenvalue describes how a transformation scales a special nonzero direction without turning it into a different line. This diagonal transformation doubles horizontal components and halves vertical ones, so the coordinate axes reveal two special behaviors.

The horizontal direction has eigenvalue two, while the vertical direction has eigenvalue one half. An ordinary diagonal vector has both components changed differently and usually turns. An eigenvalue belongs to a transformation together with an associated eigendirection.

Rearranging the eigenvector equation gives A minus lambda times the identity acting on a nonzero vector to produce zero. That requires a singular matrix. Setting its determinant to zero finds the candidate eigenvalues two and one half.

Zero can also be an eigenvalue. A singular transformation collapses some nonzero vectors to zero, and those vectors satisfy the eigenvalue equation with lambda zero. The input eigenvector must be nonzero; the resulting output need not be.
