An expression has structure before we calculate it. In two plus three times four, the multiplication describes one quantity: three groups of four. Add the separate two only after finding that product of twelve.

Parentheses change which quantity acts as a factor. Two plus three now forms one five-object group, and four copies produce twenty. The same symbols can describe a different construction when their grouping changes.

Multiplication and division have equal priority, so evaluate them from left to right unless grouping says otherwise. Eighteen divided by three gives six, then multiplying by two gives twelve; multiplying the denominator first would change the expression.

Nested expressions are read from their inner structure outward. Square three to get nine, subtract four inside the parentheses to get five, then double the result. A fraction bar also groups its entire numerator and denominator.
