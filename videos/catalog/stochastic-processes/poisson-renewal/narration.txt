A Poisson process counts random arrivals at a constant rate with independent increments over disjoint time intervals. At rate two per hour, the expected count in one hour is two. This is an average, not a promise of exactly two events.

The count in one hour has a Poisson distribution. Its first four probabilities correspond to zero, one, two, and three arrivals, with more possible counts beyond those shown. The chance of zero arrivals is e to the negative two.

The waiting time to the first arrival exceeds t exactly when no arrivals occur by t. That gives exponential survival and mean waiting time one half hour. Independent exponential interarrival times are one way to construct this Poisson process.

Exponential waiting is memoryless: elapsed waiting does not change the remaining-time distribution. A general renewal process only assumes identically distributed independent interarrival times. Fixed half-hour intervals have the same average rate but are not memoryless and do not form a Poisson process.
