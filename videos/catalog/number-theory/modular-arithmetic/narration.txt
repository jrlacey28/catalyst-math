Congruence means two integers differ by a multiple of the modulus. Fourteen and two differ by twelve, so they represent the same remainder class. We can add and multiply classes consistently, but division needs an extra condition.

Seven is invertible modulo twelve because forty-nine leaves remainder one. Its inverse is therefore seven itself. This works because seven and twelve have greatest common divisor one; an arbitrary nonzero remainder need not be invertible.

Multiply the congruence seven x equals five by the inverse seven. The left side becomes x and the right becomes thirty-five, equivalent to eleven. Checking seven times eleven gives seventy-seven, whose remainder modulo twelve is five.

Modulo six, every product of two with an integer has an even remainder. It can never equal the odd remainder one. A failed inverse does not permit formal cancellation; depending on the target, a congruence may have no solutions or several.
