phase_0
For equally likely outcomes, probability is favorable outcomes divided by all outcomes. The equally likely condition matters. A fair die has six equally likely faces, so three even faces give probability one half.

phase_1
For successive events, multiply the first probability by the conditional probability of the second. In a bag with three red and two blue balls, drawing two reds without replacement has probability three fifths times two fourths.

phase_2
At least one red includes exactly one red and two reds. It is often easier to exclude the opposite event: no red. Two blues have probability two fifths times one fourth. Subtract this from one.

phase_3
Add probabilities for disjoint alternatives. If alternatives overlap, subtract the overlap once. Multiplication and addition are chosen from the event structure, not from keywords alone. Name the event before calculating.
