phase_0
A logarithm asks which exponent produces a number. Since two cubed is eight, log base two of eight is three. These are two ways to express the same relationship, not separate arithmetic tricks.

phase_1
For real logarithms, the base is positive and not one, and the argument is positive. The exponential and logarithm graphs reflect across the diagonal because input and output swap roles.

phase_2
Multiplying equal-base powers adds their exponents. That is why the logarithm of a product becomes a sum of logarithms. The quotient rule subtracts, and a power becomes a multiplier. These rules require positive arguments in the real setting.

phase_3
To solve two to the x equals ten, take logs and divide by log of two. This is also the change-of-base formula. Do not split a logarithm of a sum: there is no corresponding sum rule.
