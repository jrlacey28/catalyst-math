phase_0
A circle consists of points a fixed distance from its center. That distance is the radius. The diameter crosses the center and has twice the radius. Changing the radius changes the whole circle.

phase_1
Imagine unrolling the boundary into a straight length. The circumference is two pi times the radius. Pi is the constant ratio of circumference to diameter, so this is also pi times the diameter.

phase_2
An arc with central angle ninety degrees is one quarter of the boundary. For an angle measured in degrees, multiply the full circumference by the angle divided by three hundred sixty.

phase_3
The same fraction gives a sector of the disk. Use pi r squared for the whole area, then multiply by the angle fraction. Arc length uses length units; sector area uses square units.
